What comes first

When you open Script Kit, several things could be what you mean: text you selected, something you just copied, the window you came from, and what you just ran (which ↑ brings back). Each wants the top. Below is one rule for choosing, and five ways to show the choice. Play the stories, or make things happen yourself, and watch where each one ends up.

The rule

  1. 1
    Anything that needs you comes first, and isn’t ranked. A run about to go, a question waiting for an answer, a failure you haven’t seen. It takes the status place, and Stop is always there.
    ⌘Z
  2. 2
    Past and present don’t compete. What you’re pointing at now becomes “this”: the input’s icon and the first rows. What you already did reports in the status place instead. They never fight for the same spot.
    Space
  3. 3
    Among the present, the newest thing you did on purpose leads. Selecting text, copying something and switching windows are all moments. The latest one is “this”. The window you came from is the floor: it leads when nothing newer does.
    ⇥
  4. 4
    News fades, and ignored news steps back. A copy leads for two minutes; a selection while it’s selected. If you opened Script Kit, saw it and did something else, it stops leading. Its key still works.
    ⌘V
  5. 5
    What you did steps behind you. The last run shows in the status place for two minutes, while ⌘Z can take it back. After that it waits on ↑; press ↑ again for earlier runs, like a terminal’s history.
    ↑

Two small rules keep it calm: only the leader gets rows (anything else still fresh shows just its name and key), and the same text selected and copied counts once.

    Try it

    • Pick Everything at once. A run is waiting, you selected an error, you copied a link and you just ran a reminder. Switch designs and see where each one lands.
    • Pick Just copied a link, then press Wait 2 min. The link stops leading and the window takes over. Switch the rule to Fixed order and the old link keeps the top.
    • Press Glance and close to open Script Kit and leave without acting. What you saw steps back next time.
    • On an empty input: Space acts on “this”, ⇥ picks the next one, ⌘V acts on what you copied, ↑ brings back the last run and ↑ again goes further back. Esc lets go. Hold ⌘ to see every key.
    • Type explain this or summarize the link. The underlined word shows what your sentence is about.

    Browsers keep some shortcuts for themselves. Click a row to select it; double-click runs it.
