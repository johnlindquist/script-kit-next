Switch the subject

When you open Script Kit, the subjects you might mean are tabs: what you selected, what you copied, the window you came from and what you just ran. Press ⇥ to move to the next one, and the rows below become what you can do with it. These variations use the same subjects and keys, but put the tabs in different places and draw them in different ways.

How the tabs behave in every variation

  1. ⇥ ⇧⇥
    Move between subjects. On an empty input, ⇥ goes to the next subject and ⇧⇥ goes back. Once you type, ⇥ keeps its current job of handing your sentence to the agent.
  2. ⌘V ↑
    Jump straight to one. ⌘V goes to what you copied. ↑ goes to the last run; press it again to step back through earlier runs, like a terminal’s history.
  3. ↵ Space
    Act on it. ↵ runs the highlighted row on the selected subject. Space puts the subject in the input, so you can say what to do with it.
  4. Esc
    Go back. Esc lets go of a subject in the input, clears your words, then returns to the subject the window opened on.
  5. It opens on the newest thing you did on purpose. When nothing is newer, it opens on the window you came from. A gold dot marks a subject that’s new since you last looked. Hold ⌘ to see each tab’s own key.

    Try it

    • Pick Everything at once, click the input and press ⇥ a few times. Then switch variations and do it again.
    • Press ↑ to jump to the last run, then ↑ again to go back through earlier ones. Esc returns to where the window opened.
    • Switch Tab order to Same place every time. The tabs stop moving as things happen, and an old copy stays as a dim tab instead of dropping off.
    • Use Make something happen to copy, select, switch apps or wait, and watch which tab the window opens on.
    • Type explain this: the sentence is about the selected tab.

    Browsers keep some shortcuts for themselves. Click a tab or a row to select it; double-click runs a row.
